---
title: Guinness bangers and mash
tags: ["irish", "sausage", "main"]
---

For 2 to 3 people.

### Sausages & Guinness onion gravy

- Thick pork sausages — Cumberland, Lincolnshire, or coarse Bratwurst (*grobe Bratwurst*): 4 to 6
- Yellow onions: 2 large (thinly sliced)
- Garlic: 2 cloves (minced)
- Guinness (or other dry Irish stout): 1.5 cups (350 ml)
- Beef broth: 1 cup (240 ml)
- Unsalted butter: 30 g (2 tbsp, plus 1 tbsp cold butter to finish)
- Neutral oil: 1 tbsp
- All-purpose flour: 2 tbsp
- Brown sugar: 1 tsp
- Worcestershire sauce: 1 tbsp
- Dijon or wholegrain mustard: 1 tsp
- Fresh thyme: 2 sprigs
- Bay leaf: 1
- Apple cider vinegar or balsamic vinegar: 1 tsp (optional, to balance)
- Salt & freshly cracked black pepper: to taste

### Buttermilk mash

- Floury potatoes (DE: *mehligkochend* / EN: Russet, Yukon Gold, Maris Piper): 1 kg (peeled and cut into even 4 cm chunks)
- Buttermilk: 1 cup (240 ml, gently warmed)
- Unsalted butter: 60 g (cut into cubes)
- Nutmeg: 1 pinch (freshly grated)
- Fresh chives or scallions: 2 tbsp (finely chopped, optional)
- Salt & freshly cracked black pepper: to taste

### Preparation

1. Place the potato chunks in a large pot, cover with cold generously salted water, and bring to a boil over high heat. Reduce to a steady simmer and cook for 15–20 minutes until fork-tender.
1. Meanwhile, heat the oil in a large heavy-bottomed skillet or Dutch oven over medium heat. Brown the sausages slowly on all sides for 8–10 minutes until deeply golden (they do not need to be fully cooked through yet). Transfer to a plate and keep the fat in the pan.
1. Add 30 g of butter to the same pan over medium-low heat. Add the sliced onions with a pinch of salt and cook slowly for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally and scraping up the sausage fond, until deeply golden and jammy.
1. Stir in the brown sugar and minced garlic, and cook for 1–2 minutes until fragrant.
1. Sprinkle the flour over the onions and stir constantly for 2 minutes to cook out the raw flour taste.
1. Pour in 1 cup (240 ml) of Guinness while scraping the bottom of the pan to deglaze. Let it bubble for 2 minutes to cook off the alcohol, then stir in the beef broth, Worcestershire sauce, mustard, thyme sprigs, and bay leaf.
1. Bring the gravy to a simmer, then nestle the sausages (and any juices on the plate) back into the pan, leaving the tops slightly exposed so the skins stay browned. Pour in the remaining 1/2 cup (110 ml) of Guinness if needed, partially cover, and simmer gently for 10–12 minutes until the sausages are cooked through and the gravy is rich and glossy.
1. While the sausages finish, drain the cooked potatoes thoroughly and return them to the hot, dry pot over very low heat for 1–2 minutes to let excess steam evaporate.
1. Mash the potatoes (or pass through a potato ricer), then fold in the cubed butter until melted. Gradually stir in the gently warmed buttermilk until smooth and fluffy — do not overheat the buttermilk or it may curdle. Season with salt, black pepper, freshly grated nutmeg, and optional chives.
1. Remove the thyme sprigs and bay leaf from the gravy. Turn off the heat, swirl in 1 tbsp of cold butter for a velvety shine, and taste — add a splash of vinegar, salt, or black pepper as needed to balance the stout's bitterness.
1. Spoon a generous bed of buttermilk mash onto warm plates, top with the sausages, and ladle the Guinness onion gravy over everything. Serve immediately, ideally with buttered green peas or sautéed cabbage.

:::tip[Tips & Variations]
- **Don't boil the Guinness too hard**: Stout concentrates its bitterness when aggressively reduced. Using deeply caramelized onions, a touch of brown sugar, Worcestershire sauce, and a final knob of cold butter balances it into a rich, savory pub gravy.
- **Steam-dry your potatoes**: Letting drained potatoes sit in the hot pot for a minute before mashing drives off moisture so they absorb way more butter and buttermilk without turning watery. Never use a blender or food processor, which makes mash gluey.
- **Irish Champ twist**: Stirring finely chopped scallions (spring onions) into the buttermilk mash turns it into classic Irish *champ*, which pairs brilliantly with the rich gravy.
- **Extra-crispy sausages**: If you prefer snappy, un-braised skins, cook the sausages all the way through in a separate skillet (deglazing with a splash of Guinness at the end) and spoon the gravy over them right before serving.
:::
