## Ingredients

### Salmon & Prep

- Salmon fillets (skin-on): 300g
- Salt: 2 pinches
- Potato starch (Katakuriko) or cornstarch: 1 tbsp
- Cooking oil: 1 tbsp
- Green onions (finely chopped, for garnish): As needed
- Toasted sesame oil (for finishing): As needed

### Miso Glaze

- Yellow miso paste (mix of white and red): 1 tbsp
- Sake (or dry white wine / water): 1 tbsp
- Grated garlic: 1 tsp
- Japanese soy sauce (Koikuchi shoyu): 1 tsp
- Light brown sugar: 1 tsp

## Instructions

1. **Prepare the Miso Glaze:** In a small bowl, combine the yellow miso paste, sake, grated garlic, soy sauce, and light brown sugar. Stir thoroughly until smooth.
1. **Prep the Salmon:** Pat the salmon fillets dry. Season them with salt, then lightly coat all sides with potato starch (or cornstarch), shaking off any excess. This helps create a crispy exterior and makes the glaze adhere.
1. **Pan-fry the Salmon:** Heat the cooking oil in a pan over medium heat. Place the salmon fillets in the pan, skin-side down. Sauté until the skin is golden and crispy during 3 minutes, then flip and cook until the fish is just cooked through (1-2 minutes).
1. **Apply the Glaze:**
   - Turn off the heat completely (miso and sugar burn very easily).
   - Pour or brush the miso glaze over the salmon fillets. Use the residual heat of the pan to gently caramelize and evenly coat the fish.
1. **Plate and Serve:** Transfer the salmon to a plate, skin-side up. Garnish with finely chopped green onions and a light drizzle of toasted sesame oil. Serve immediately.

## Notes

- **The "Rice Balancer" Principle:** In Japanese home cooking, dishes are often seasoned boldly because they are meant to be eaten alongside plain steamed white rice. If serving without rice (e.g., with a salad), consider slightly reducing the miso and soy sauce.
- **Role of Sake:** Sake is used to eliminate fishy odors, tenderize the fish, and add a subtle depth of umami.
- **Glaze Tip:** Always apply the glaze with the heat off. Miso and sugar scorch rapidly, so "painting with residual heat" is the key to a perfect glaze.
