---
title: Mapo Tofu
tags: ["chinese", "tofu", "sichuan"]
---

## Ingredients

- Tofu (medium-firm or soft): 450g
- Ground pork (or beef): 150g
- Pixian doubanjiang (fermented chili bean paste): 2 tbsp (30g)
- Fermented black beans (douchi): 1 tbsp (15g)
- Sichuan peppercorns (toasted and ground): 1 tsp
- Garlic: 3 cloves
- Ginger: 1 thumb-sized piece
- Scallions: 2 stalks
- Chicken stock (or water): 1 cup (240ml)
- Light soy sauce: 1 tbsp (15ml)
- Shaoxing wine: 1 tbsp (15ml)
- Sugar: 1 tsp (5g)
- Cornstarch: 1 tbsp (10g)
- Cold water (for slurry): 2 tbsp (30ml)
- Chili oil: 1 tbsp (15ml)
- Neutral oil: 2 tbsp (30ml)
- Salt (for parboiling): 1 tsp

## Method

1. Cut the tofu into 2 cm (3/4 inch) cubes.
1. Bring a pot of water with 1 tsp of salt to a gentle simmer. Gently slide in the tofu cubes and simmer for 1 to 2 minutes. Carefully drain the tofu and keep warm; parboiling firms the tofu to prevent breaking and removes raw bean taste.
1. Toast the Sichuan peppercorns in a dry skillet over low heat until fragrant (about 1 minute). Grind into a fine powder using a mortar and pestle or spice grinder, and set aside.
1. Finely mince the garlic, ginger, and fermented black beans (douchi). Finely chop the doubanjiang so there are no large bean pieces. Slice the scallions, keeping the white and green parts separate.
1. In a small bowl, whisk together the cornstarch and cold water to make a cornstarch slurry.
1. Heat the neutral oil in a wok or deep skillet over medium-high heat. Add the ground pork (or beef) and stir-fry, breaking it apart into small bits until browned and slightly crisp.
1. Lower the heat to medium-low. Add the doubanjiang and stir-fry for about 1 minute until the oil turns a rich red color.
1. Add the fermented black beans, minced garlic, minced ginger, and the white parts of the scallions. Stir-fry for 30 to 45 seconds until aromatic.
1. Pour in the chicken stock, Shaoxing wine, light soy sauce, and sugar. Stir well and bring to a simmer.
1. Gently slide the tofu cubes into the sauce. Gently push the tofu around with the back of a spatula to avoid breaking the cubes. Simmer on medium-low heat for 3 to 5 minutes so the tofu absorbs the sauce.
1. Give the cornstarch slurry a stir to recombine. Drizzle about one-third of the slurry around the wok, gently pushing the tofu and sauce. Wait 30 seconds for the sauce to thicken, then add the remaining slurry in 1 or 2 additions until the sauce is glossy and coats the back of a spoon.
1. Drizzle with the chili oil and gently swirl to combine.
1. Turn off the heat and transfer the mapo tofu to a shallow serving bowl. Sprinkle generously with the ground Sichuan pepper and sliced green scallions. Serve immediately with steamed white rice.
