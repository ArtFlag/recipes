## Traditional method

Ingrédients : Pour 1,5 à 2 litres de bouillon

- 1 kilo de carcasse de volaille ou d'os à moelle (boeuf, veau)
- 3 litres d'eau
- Optionel: 2 branches de celeri
- 90g poireau
- 90g carottes
- 90g oignon
- 2 garlic cloves

Aromatiques:

- 1 feuilles de laurier
- 2 branches de thym
- 8 grains de poivres

Méthode:

1. Désosser le poulet au niveau de chaque articulation.
1. Rincer les os.
1. (Optionel) Mettre sur un plaque, arroser d'un peu d'huile et enfourner pour 30 minutes à 190.
1. Mettre dans une marmite. Recouvrir d'eau et faire mijoter doucement sans couvrir.
   :::tip
   Placer le pot a moitié au dessus du feu pour avoir une convection excentree.
   :::
1. Apres qq minutes enlever le scum.
1. Apres qq minutes ajouter qq glaçons pour extraire le gras.
1. **Faire mijoter 90 minutes.**

   :::note
   Ca doit mijiter, pas bouillir. Ne pas melanger pour eviter de remuer les particules au fond. Faire bouillir rend le bouillon trouble.

   Pour faire un fond, laisser mijoter 4h.
   :::

1. Peler et couper les legumes.
1. **Ajouter le mirepoix et les aromatiques et laisser mijoter 30-40 minutes.**
1. Extraire le bouillon avec une louche.
1. Mettre dans un bain de glace et mettre au frigo.

## Quick pressure-cooker chicken broth (Chris Young)

Based on Chris Young's *Pressure Roast Chicken Stock* method: under 15 minutes of active prep (~10 minutes), followed by 30–90 minutes in a pressure cooker. Yields about 2 L.

Ingredients:

- Store-bought rotisserie chicken: 1
- Yellow onion (thinly sliced): 150 (g)
- Carrot (thinly sliced): 75 (g)
- Garlic (crushed): 2 (cloves)
- Neutral oil or chicken fat: as needed
- Water: 2 (L)

Method:

1. Heat enough neutral oil or chicken fat to coat the bottom of a pressure cooker over medium-high heat.
1. Add the thinly sliced onion, carrot, and crushed garlic, and let them sear undisturbed until deeply browned at the edges to build fond.
1. While the aromatics sear, pull the meat off the rotisserie chicken, separating the bones, skin, and cartilage (reserve the meat for other dishes, or finely shred it and include it for extra richness).
1. Add the chicken bones (and skin/trimmings) to the pressure cooker with the browned aromatics, then pour in the water.
1. Seal the lid and cook on **high pressure for 30 to 90 minutes**:
   - **30 minutes** (roughly 2–3 hours of stovetop simmering) for a quick, flavorful broth.
   - **60–90 minutes** (roughly 5–8 hours of simmering) for a rich, gelatinous stock.
1. Allow the pressure cooker to cool down and release pressure naturally without venting.

   :::tip
   Letting pressure release naturally prevents boiling turbulence and keeps the broth clear. Avoid pressure-cooking chicken bones past 3–4 hours, as over-extraction degrades gelatin and can create bitter or metallic flavors.
   :::

1. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve and chill promptly.

## 15-minute veal broth (Chris Young)

Veal is neutral in flavor, so this rapid clarified broth can be used in any dish.

Ingredients:

- Ground veal: 450 (g)
- Onion (chopped): 1/2
- Carrot (chopped): 1/4
- Celery stalk (chopped): 1/4
- Garlic (chopped): 1 (clove)
- Oil: as needed
- Gelatin: 7 (g) (1 packet)
- Water: 1000 (g) (divided: 500 g for deglazing, 500 g for the blender)
- Egg white: 1
- Bay leaf: 1
- Thyme: to taste
- Peppercorns: to taste

Method:

1. In a pan with enough oil, brown both sides of the block of ground veal before breaking it up (about 5 minutes).
1. Once browned, break the meat up a little bit at a time.
1. Prep the mirepoix: chop the 1/2 onion, 1/4 carrot, 1/4 celery stalk, and 1 garlic clove.
1. Drain excess oil from the meat, return the pan to the burner, add the vegetables, and let them brown off.
1. Once fond has formed, add the 7 g packet of gelatin and 500 g of water to the meat, and deglaze the pan.
1. Bring back to a gentle simmer.
1. In a blender, add 500 g of water and 1 egg white, and blend at high speed for a few seconds to break up the albumin.
1. Pour the meat stock into the blender.
1. Blend until the stock is murky (do not blend into a smoothie).
1. Return to the pot, add the bay leaf, thyme, and peppercorns, and let simmer for 2–3 minutes.
1. Cover the pan with a lid for 30 seconds to fully cook the egg white.

Reduce to a quarter or a fifth for a demi-glace.
