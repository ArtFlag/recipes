---
title: Apple Butter
tags: ["american", "spread", "apple"]
---

Makes about 350–400 ml (1 to 2 jars).

- Apples: 1 kg (cored and roughly chopped — peeling optional if blending well; see varieties below)
- Water: 60 ml (just enough to prevent sticking until the apples release their juice)
- Brown sugar (*brauner Zucker* or Muscovado): 75 g (adjust to taste)
- Apple cider vinegar (*Apfelessig*) or lemon juice: 1 tbsp
- Ground cinnamon: 1 tsp
- Ground nutmeg: 1/4 tsp
- Ground cloves: 1/8 tsp (a pinch)
- Ground allspice (*Piment*) or ground ginger: 1/8 tsp (a pinch)
- Vanilla extract: 1/2 tsp
- Salt: 1/8 tsp (a pinch)

1. Place the chopped apples and water in a heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven. Cover and cook over medium-low heat for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the apples are completely soft and breaking down.
1. Purée the apples until completely smooth using an immersion blender (or pass through a fine sieve/food mill if you left the skins on and want an ultra-silky texture).
1. Stir in the brown sugar, apple cider vinegar, cinnamon, nutmeg, cloves, allspice (or ginger), vanilla extract, and salt. Taste and adjust sugar or spices if needed.
1. Cook down slowly until thick, glossy, and deep caramel-brown using one of two methods:
   - **Oven (recommended — less stirring and splattering):** Transfer the uncovered Dutch oven to the oven <BakingStep temp="150" time="1.5 to 2 hours" preheat />, stirring every 20 to 30 minutes and scraping down the sides.
   - **Stovetop:** Simmer uncovered on the lowest heat for 1 to 1.5 hours, stirring frequently to prevent scorching (use a splatter screen if it bubbles).
1. Check doneness by placing a spoonful on a cold plate: it should hold a thick mound with no watery ring seeping out around the edges.
1. Spoon into clean jars, let cool to room temperature, then refrigerate. Keeps for 3 to 4 weeks in the fridge, or up to 6 months in the freezer.

:::tip[Best Apple Varieties in Germany]
For the richest flavor, combine **tart, soft cooking apples** (which break down quickly and keep the spread bright) with **sweet, aromatic apples** (for natural sweetness and depth):

- **Tart / soft base (50–70%):**
  - **Boskoop (*Schöner aus Boskoop*)**: The classic German baking and *Apfelmus* apple — very tart, fragrant, and melts into purée effortlessly (available autumn through winter).
  - **Elstar**: Easy to find year-round in German supermarkets (*REWE, Edeka, Lidl, Aldi*); balanced sweet-tart flavor and breaks down well.
  - **Jonagold**: Soft flesh, sweet-tart, gives great body.
  - **Holsteiner Cox**: Very aromatic and mildly tart; great if you find it at a *Wochenmarkt*.
- **Sweet / aromatic boost (30–50%):**
  - **Braeburn**: Sweet-tart with warm, spicy notes (firmer, so best paired with Boskoop or Elstar).
  - **Gala** or **Fuji**: High natural sweetness, allowing you to cut back slightly on added sugar.
  - **Topaz** or **Rubinette** (*Wochenmarkt* / *Bioladen*): Intense, complex apple flavor.
- **Easy supermarket combos:** **50% Boskoop + 50% Elstar**, or **50% Elstar + 50% Jonagold** when Boskoop is out of season.
- **Skin on vs. peeled:** Leaving the skins on adds pectin (thicker texture) and deeper color, as long as you blend thoroughly or pass through a sieve.
:::
