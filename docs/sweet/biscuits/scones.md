Tall, ultra-flaky, tender scones made with grated frozen butter and 5 simple lamination folds. Chilling the cut dough right before baking guarantees dramatic oven spring and crisp, golden edges.

Yields 8–10 scones.

## Ingredients

### Dough

- All-purpose flour: 360g (3 cups)
- Granulated sugar: 65g (⅓ cup)
- Baking powder: 15g (1 tbsp)
- Baking soda: 2g (½ tsp)
- Fine sea salt: 5g (1 tsp)
- Unsalted butter (frozen): 115g (1 stick / ½ cup)
- Buttermilk (cold): 150ml (⅔ cup), plus 1–2 tbsp if needed
- Egg (cold): 1 large
- Vanilla extract: 5ml (1 tsp)
- Mix-ins (optional): 100g (⅔ cup) currants, raisins, dried cranberries, or chocolate chips, plus zest of 1 lemon or orange

### Topping

- Heavy cream (or milk): 30ml (2 tbsp)
- Coarse sugar (demerara or turbinado): 1–2 tbsp (optional)

## Preparation

1. Freeze the butter for at least 30 minutes (or overnight) until rock solid.
1. In a large bowl, whisk together:
   - All-purpose flour
   - Granulated sugar
   - Baking powder
   - Baking soda
   - Fine sea salt
   - Citrus zest (if using)
1. Coarsely grate the frozen butter on the large holes of a box grater directly into the bowl (or onto a chilled plate), tossing the shreds in the flour mixture every few strokes so they stay separate and coated. If using optional mix-ins, toss them in now.
1. In a small bowl or measuring jug, whisk together the cold buttermilk, cold egg, and vanilla extract.
1. Pour the wet mixture into the dry ingredients and gently toss with a fork or rubber spatula just until a shaggy dough forms with a few dry floury patches remaining. If the dough feels too dry to hold together, add 1–2 tablespoons of cold buttermilk. Do not overmix.
1. Turn the shaggy dough and any loose crumbs onto a lightly floured work surface and gently press into a rough rectangle about 2cm thick.
1. Laminate the dough with 5 half-folds to build flaky layers:
   - Fold the dough in half, rotate it 90°, and gently pat or roll it back into a 2cm-thick rectangle.
   - Repeat this fold-rotate-press motion 4 more times, for a total of 5 folds. (If the butter starts feeling soft at any point, chill the dough in the fridge for 10 minutes before continuing.)
1. Gently pat the dough to a uniform thickness of 2.5–3cm.
1. Cut the scones:
   - For classic rounds: press a floured 6–7cm round cutter straight down into the dough without twisting (twisting seals the edges and limits rise). Gently press scraps together once to cut the last scones.
   - For zero-scrap scones: cut the rectangle into 8 squares or triangles with a sharp knife or bench scraper.
1. Arrange the scones on a baking tray lined with parchment paper, spaced about 3–4cm apart. Place the tray in the freezer for 15 minutes while the oven preheats (this relaxes the gluten and re-chills the butter for maximum lift).
1. Brush only the tops of the scones with heavy cream (avoid dripping down the cut sides, which can seal the layers) and sprinkle with coarse sugar if using.
1. <BakingStep temp="200 C / 400 F" time="15–18 minutes" preheat /> (or 190 C / 375 F with fan) until tall, deeply golden on top, and firm at the edges.
1. Let cool on the tray for 5 minutes before serving warm with clotted cream, butter, and jam.
