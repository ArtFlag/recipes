The authentic Russian and Armenian *Торт Наполеон* (*Նապոլեոն*). Unlike the crisp French mille-feuille, this traditional version features 8 paper-thin, blistered pastry layers softened by a rich custard cream (*zavarnoy krem*) whipped with butter and sweetened condensed milk (*sgushchyonka*). After resting overnight, it yields an iconic melt-in-the-mouth texture.

## Pastry dough (for 8 layers)

- All-purpose flour: 350 g (plus extra for rolling)
- Unsalted butter (well-chilled or frozen): 250 g
- Ice water: 100 ml
- Egg (cold): 1
- Vodka or Armenian cognac: 1 tbsp (15 ml)
- White vinegar: 1/2 tbsp (7.5 ml)
- Fine salt: 1/2 tsp (3 g)

## Custard cream

- Whole milk: 750 ml
- Eggs: 3
- Granulated sugar: 150 g
- Cornstarch: 30 g (2 tbsp)
- All-purpose flour: 30 g (2 tbsp)
- Sweetened condensed milk (*sgushchyonka*): 200 g (about 1/2 can)
- Unsalted butter (softened at room temperature): 250 g
- Vanilla extract: 2 tsp (10 ml)
- Armenian cognac or brandy (optional): 1 tbsp (15 ml)

## 1. Prepare the flaky pastry dough

1. In a measuring cup, whisk together the ice water, cold egg, vinegar, vodka (or cognac), and salt. Keep chilled in the refrigerator.
1. Sift the flour into a large, wide mixing bowl.
1. Dip the block of cold butter into the flour and coarsely grate it directly into the bowl with a box grater, tossing the butter shreds in the flour frequently so they do not clump together.
1. Make a well in the center of the flour-butter mixture and pour in the cold liquid.
1. Quickly gather the dough together with your hands just until it forms a cohesive mass. Do not knead, as the butter flakes must stay intact to create layers.
1. Divide the dough into 8 equal balls (about 90–95 g each).
1. Place the dough balls on a floured plate, cover tightly with plastic wrap, and refrigerate for at least 2 hours (or overnight).

## 2. Prepare the custard cream (*zavarnoy krem*)

1. In a medium saucepan, heat the milk over medium heat until steaming, just below a simmer.
1. In a heatproof bowl, whisk the eggs and granulated sugar until smooth and pale.
1. Whisk in the flour and cornstarch until completely lump-free.
1. Slowly pour about a third of the warm milk into the egg mixture in a steady stream while whisking continuously to temper the eggs.
1. Pour the tempered mixture back into the saucepan with the remaining milk.
1. Cook over medium-low heat, whisking constantly and scraping the bottom and corners of the pan, until the custard thickens and bubbles burst at the surface. Boil gently for 1 minute while whisking vigorously.
1. Remove from heat, stir in the vanilla extract, and immediately transfer the custard to a clean bowl.
1. Press plastic wrap directly onto the surface of the custard and allow it to cool completely to room temperature (it must match the temperature of the butter).
1. In a large bowl, beat the softened room-temperature butter with a hand mixer or stand mixer for 4 to 5 minutes until pale, light, and fluffy.
1. Gradually pour in the sweetened condensed milk while continuing to beat.
1. Add the cooled custard base one generous spoonful at a time, beating well after each addition until a smooth, velvety, and silky cream forms.
1. Beat in the cognac or brandy, if using.

## 3. Roll and bake the pastry layers

1. Take one dough ball from the refrigerator at a time, leaving the others chilled.
1. Place a sheet of parchment paper on your work surface, dust lightly with flour, and roll out the dough ball directly on the paper as thinly as possible (less than 1.5 mm thick).
1. Place a round cake ring, tart ring, or 22 cm (9-inch) plate on top and cut around it with a small knife or pizza cutter. Leave the trimmings directly on the paper alongside the disc.
1. Prick the pastry circle all over with a fork so it blisters instead of puffing into a large balloon.
1. Slide the parchment paper onto a baking sheet.

1. Slide the baked disc onto a cooling rack.
1. Repeat the rolling and baking process for all 8 dough balls and their trimmings.
1. Once completely cooled, collect all the baked trimmings (plus one less-than-perfect pastry disc if desired) in a bowl and crush them by hand into fine, flaky crumbs for the coating.

## 4. Assemble and rest the cake

1. Dab a tablespoon of cream in the center of your serving plate to anchor the cake.
1. Place the first pastry disc on the plate and spread 3 to 4 generous tablespoons of custard cream evenly over it.
1. Place the next pastry layer on top, press down gently and evenly with your palms to slightly settle it, and spread with cream.
1. Repeat with all remaining layers, reserving about 4 to 5 tablespoons of cream for the finish.
1. Spread the remaining cream evenly over the top and around the sides of the cake.
1. Generously cover the entire cake with the crushed pastry crumbs, pressing them gently onto the sides and scattering them across the top.
1. Let the assembled cake sit at cool room temperature for 2 hours to jump-start cream absorption, then transfer to the refrigerator for at least 12 hours (ideally 24 hours).
1. Slice with a sharp knife and serve chilled or slightly cool.
